Public & Regulatory Legal Services

Public & Regulatory legal services in Saudi Arabia. Riyadh, Jeddah & Dammam.

A company's relationship with government regulatory bodies extends well beyond standard commercial registration, into additional sector-specific licenses, regulatory compliance reviews, and representation before ministries and authorities when a transaction or approval needs direct follow-up, or when a compliance issue arises.

A few situations come up repeatedly. A company entering a new activity that requires an additional sector license beyond general commercial registration. A company receiving an inquiry or notice from a regulatory body regarding a specific obligation. A foreign investor needing to track ongoing MISA obligations after the initial license is issued. Or a company planning a material change in its activity that needs the regulatory impact assessed before implementation.

The applicable regulatory framework varies by sector and activity, and a company may fall under Ministry of Commerce regulations alongside a specialized sector regulator, such as the Capital Market Authority for financial activities or the Communications, Space and Technology Commission for telecom activities. The company's actual activity determines exactly which regulatory body or bodies apply.

Engagements begin by mapping the company's complete regulatory picture, not just its current licenses, but those a planned expansion or new activity might trigger, so required approvals are arranged in advance rather than discovered one by one during implementation.

When a regulatory body raises an informal inquiry before any formal notice, we treat it with the same seriousness as a formal procedure, since an early, well-managed response often prevents the matter from escalating to something more formal in the first place.

Periodic compliance review, even just annually, is far less costly over the long term than only engaging a lawyer after the regulator has already identified a violation.

We often see companies lose government tenders not over the quality of their technical proposal, but over a small formal requirement in the tender document, like an expired classification certificate or a financial document in the wrong format, which excludes the bid entirely before it even reaches technical evaluation. Government-related matters connect directly with our government contracts and public procurement service, and professional license matters fall under our professional licensing and disciplinary service.

Common Questions

How do we know which regulatory body applies to our specific activity?

We review the company's actual activity to determine the applicable regulatory body or bodies, since some activities fall under more than one body's jurisdiction at once.

What do we do if we receive an inquiry from a regulatory body without formal notice?

We recommend treating it with the same seriousness as a formal procedure, since an early, well-managed response often prevents the matter from escalating.

Do you assess the regulatory impact of a planned expansion before it's implemented?

Yes, we assess this specifically to identify any additional licenses the expansion might trigger, and arrange for these in advance rather than discovering them during implementation.

Do you track ongoing MISA obligations after a license is issued?

Yes, these obligations continue and don't end with the initial license, and we track them to ensure the company stays fully compliant with its terms.
